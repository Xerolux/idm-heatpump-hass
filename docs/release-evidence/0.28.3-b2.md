## Final Verdict

### Reference-plant DHW boost, 2026-11-20

The Navigator 10 was heating in Automatic with both heating circuit pumps
running. The upper tank measured 51.3 °C, the normal switch-off threshold
was 50 °C, and the original DHW setpoint was 48 °C. One integration boost
request for 53 °C with a five-minute timeout returned HTTP 200. Register
1713 read back as 1 during the observation without a reported write error.
The controller stayed in Automatic, the circuit modes stayed Normal, and
both circuit pumps remained on. The valve stayed on heating, normal DHW
demand stayed off, and the DHW energy counter did not increase. The
compressor transitioned from heating to standby during the observation.
After the timeout, the original 48 °C setpoint was observed and the cancel
button reported the boost inactive. The final state of register 1713 could
not be checked after the Home Assistant API became unreachable. A physical
DHW charge remains unverified. Repeat the test with a cooler tank.

### Repeat test with cooler tank

The tank was 46.98 °C at the top, between the normal 46 °C switch-on and
50 °C switch-off thresholds. Before the boost, the controller was switched
from heating/cooling only to Automatic and normal DHW demand remained off.
The 50 °C boost caused register 1713 to read back as 1. During the five-minute
test, Automatic and both heating circuit pumps remained active. The unit
stayed in heating, the valve did not switch, and the DHW counter remained
4051.99 kWh. After timeout, register 1032 read 48 °C and the boost was
inactive. Register 1713 still read 1 at the final poll. No physical DHW
charge was observed even with the tank below the switch-off threshold.
